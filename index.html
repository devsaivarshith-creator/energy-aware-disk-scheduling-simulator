How Hard Disk Scheduling Works (Simple Guide)

A Hard Disk has spinning circular tracks and a mechanical arm that moves back and forth to read data. Moving the arm uses mechanical energy and takes time. Disk scheduling algorithms decide the smartest order to visit requested tracks so the drive uses minimal power without making requests wait forever.

1. Track Requests Apps ask for data scattered across cylinders (e.g. 14, 98, 183).
2. Head Motion (Seek) The mechanical arm jumps between tracks, burning electric power.
3. Energy Trade-off Greedy algorithms save motion but starve far tracks. Energy-Aware balances both!

Algorithm Guide: Explained in Simple Plain English
FCFS First-Come First-Served
🛒 Analogy: The Grocery Checkout Queue

Services requests strictly in the order they arrived. Extremely fair, but the head wildly swings back and forth across the disk, wasting vast amounts of mechanical energy.

✓ Simple & zero starvation ✗ Highest seek distance & energy waste
SSTF Shortest Seek Time First
🐝 Analogy: The Greedy Bee

Always jumps to whichever track is closest right now. Yields low seek time, but tracks at the far opposite ends of the disk may starve and wait forever.

✓ Very low seek distance ✗ Starvation risk for distant requests
SCAN Elevator Algorithm
🛗 Analogy: The Skyscraper Elevator

Moves continuously in one direction servicing tracks on the way until it hits the boundary edge (track 0 or N-1), then reverses direction and sweeps back.

✓ Smooth predictable motion ✗ Wastes time traveling to empty edges
C-SCAN Circular SCAN
🎡 Analogy: One-Way Luggage Carousel

Only services tracks moving in one direction. When it hits the far edge, it quickly jumps back to the opposite edge and sweeps again, giving uniform wait times.

✓ Uniform waiting time ✗ Unproductive return jump seek
LOOK Smart Elevator
🏢 Analogy: Elevator that only goes as far as passengers

Like SCAN, but looks ahead. It reverses immediately after servicing the furthest request in that direction, saving energy by never traveling to empty disk boundaries.

✓ Better energy savings than SCAN ✗ Higher wait time variance than C-LOOK
C-LOOK Circular LOOK
🔄 Analogy: Express Loop Shuttle

Like C-SCAN, but only travels between the lowest and highest actual requested tracks. Eliminates boundary travel on both ends, achieving great efficiency.

✓ Great all-around conventional algorithm ✗ Ignores request wait time in decision score
Proposed Energy-Aware Novel OS Algorithm
🚗 Analogy: Intelligent Eco-Route GPS

Evaluates a dual objective cost function: Score = α × SeekDistance - β × WaitTime. It aggressively minimizes seek distance to conserve actuator energy, while the aging wait-time factor dynamically boosts starving tracks to guarantee fairness!

✓ Optimum balance of Energy, Seek & Fairness ✓ Zero starvation with customizable α and β
